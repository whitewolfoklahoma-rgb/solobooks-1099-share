How SoloBooks 1099 works

The idea

One file, no account, no subscription, no bank login — your books never leave this device. Log income, expenses, and business miles as they happen; at quarter-end the app estimates what to send the IRS; at year-end it prints a Schedule C summary your tax preparer can copy from. Manual entry is the feature: nothing connects to your bank, ever.

Formulas used

Net profit = total income − deductible expenses − mileage deduction. Meals count at 50% (the standard limit for most business meals). The mileage deduction = business miles × the standard mileage rate saved for that year (Settings).

Self-employment tax = net profit × 92.35% × 15.3%. Half of that tax is then deducted before the federal income-tax estimate: federal estimate = (profit − half the SE tax) × your federal effective-rate %. State estimate = profit × your state %. Total = SE tax + federal + state; each quarter's suggested payment is one quarter of the cumulative annualized total.

What's NOT in these estimates

This is a planning worksheet, not a tax return and not tax advice. It leaves out: the Social Security wage-base cap on part of the SE tax, the standard deduction and real tax brackets (that's why you supply an effective rate), other income, W-2 wages, credits, retirement-plan deductions, and state quirks. If your profit is large or your situation is mixed, a tax professional will beat this worksheet — use it to avoid surprises and underpayment penalties, not as your final number.

Mileage vs. actual car costs

Pick one method per vehicle per year. If you use the standard mileage rate (what this app's Mileage tab does), you may still log parking fees and tolls under "Car & truck" — but not gas, repairs, or insurance for that vehicle. Logging both inflates your deduction; the IRS disallows it.

Meals

Most business meals are 50% deductible. Enter the full amount you paid; the app deducts half and shows both figures. Meals that are 100% deductible (e.g., office holiday parties) should be logged under "Other expenses" with a note instead.

Your data

Everything is stored in this browser's local storage on this device only. Clearing your browser's site data erases it — export backups from Settings & Backup and keep the file somewhere safe. A backup from this app can be re-imported into any copy of SoloBooks 1099.

Defaults to localize

The mileage rates and tax percentages are planning defaults, clearly labeled and editable in Settings. The IRS sets the standard mileage rate per year by notice; verify yours before filing. The federal/state percentages are your own effective-rate estimates — last year's return (tax ÷ income) is a good source.
